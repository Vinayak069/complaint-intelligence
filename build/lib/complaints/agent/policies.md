# Servicing policy notes (US credit cards)

Short summaries of public US rules that the triage agent may cite. General information for
routing and drafting only, not legal advice.

## POL-01 Billing error disputes (Fair Credit Billing Act, Regulation Z 1026.13)
Queues: Billing dispute, Escalated investigation
A cardholder may dispute a billing error (a charge they did not authorise, goods not delivered, wrong amount) by notifying the issuer in writing within 60 days of the statement that first showed it. The issuer must acknowledge within 30 days and resolve within two complete billing cycles, no more than 90 days. The cardholder does not have to pay the disputed amount, or related finance charges, while it is investigated.

## POL-02 Unauthorised use (Regulation Z 1026.12(b))
Queues: Billing dispute, Card usage
Cardholder liability for unauthorised use of a credit card is capped at $50, and is zero for charges made after the card was reported lost or stolen. Most issuers apply a zero-liability policy.

## POL-03 Purchase quality claims (Regulation Z 1026.12(c))
Queues: Billing dispute
For a purchase over $50 made in the cardholder's home state or within 100 miles of home, the cardholder may assert claims against the issuer that they have against the merchant, after a good-faith attempt to resolve it with the merchant.

## POL-04 Rate increases and fees (CARD Act, Regulation Z 1026.9 and 1026.52)
Queues: Fees and interest
Issuers must give 45 days' notice before increasing an APR or significant fees, with a right to reject the change for existing balances. Penalty fees must be reasonable and proportional to the violation; a late fee may not exceed the minimum payment that was missed.

## POL-05 Payment allocation and timing (Regulation Z 1026.10 and 1026.53)
Queues: Payments
Payments received by 5 p.m. on the due date must be credited that day. Amounts paid above the minimum must go first to the balance with the highest APR.

## POL-06 Hardship and collections
Queues: Payments
Consumers struggling to pay may ask about hardship programmes (temporary lower APR or payment plans). Collection communications must not be abusive or misleading; third-party debt collectors are covered by the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act.

## POL-07 Credit reporting accuracy (Fair Credit Reporting Act section 623)
Queues: Credit reporting
When a consumer disputes information through a credit bureau, the issuer (as furnisher) must investigate, generally within 30 days, review the information the bureau passes on, and correct or delete inaccurate data with every bureau it reported to.

## POL-08 Application decisions (Equal Credit Opportunity Act, Regulation B 1002.9)
Queues: Applications
An applicant must be told of a decision within 30 days of a completed application. A denial requires an adverse action notice giving the specific principal reasons, or saying how to ask for them.

## POL-09 Account closure
Queues: Account closure
A cardholder may close an account at any time and repay the balance under the existing terms. When an issuer closes or limits an account, it should give the reason where required and must not charge fees for the closure itself; rewards forfeiture follows the programme terms disclosed to the customer.

## POL-10 Rewards and promotional offers (UDAAP)
Queues: Rewards and offers
Advertised offers (sign-up bonuses, promotional APRs, rewards) must be honoured as disclosed. Misleading or unclear offer terms may be unfair, deceptive or abusive acts or practices (UDAAP). Review the offer terms the customer received.

## POL-11 Complaint handling standards
Queues: all
Companies respond to CFPB complaints within 15 days (a final response within 60 days). A response should address every issue raised, explain the outcome, and not promise remediation before investigation.

## POL-12 Servicemembers and older consumers
Queues: all
Servicemembers have Servicemembers Civil Relief Act (6% APR cap on pre-service debt) and Military Lending Act protections. Complaints tagged Older American or Servicemember should be reviewed for these protections and possible elder financial exploitation.
